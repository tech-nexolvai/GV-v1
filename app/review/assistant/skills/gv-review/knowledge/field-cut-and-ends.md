# Field cut and how a top meets its walls

A room is never exactly as drawn, so a countertop that runs wall to wall is made a little longer and
trimmed on site. That extra is the **field cut**. GV's own value per wall end is in `rules.md`; never
assume one.

## Three layouts, and when the field cut applies

**A. The top runs over the fillers to the walls.** The top's line reaches each wall; the fillers sit
under it. The field cut applies at each end where the top meets a wall. Top width = the pieces under
it (cabinets and fillers) + the field cut per wall end.

**B. The top sits between full-height fillers or panels.** The top's band stops short of the run's
ends; tall panels or fillers on each side meet the walls. The fillers take the fit to the wall (the
vendor's note often says "filler to be field cut"). No field cut on the top. The top is only as wide
as the pieces under it.

**C. The top sits in pockets in the walls.** The top's line passes the wall faces, or its printed
length is larger than the wall-to-wall. The top's length = the opening + the designed depth in each
pocket. No extra field cut. Without the pocket detail, a person decides.

## How to tell them apart
- A: the top's outline meets the wall lines; no tall panel at the ends.
- B: full-height panels or fillers at the ends; the top's band is shorter than the run.
- C: the top's line runs into the walls; a pocket is drawn or noted.
- A free-standing end (no wall) gets no field cut and no side splash.

## What it means for a finding
- Only layout A adds the field cut. Applying it to B or C gives false Wrongs.
- A vendor string that says "including field cut" must still add up exactly: name each part's label
  and the total's; code adds them.
- When you cannot tell the layout, report the item as not compared (status check) and say which layout is unclear.
