# Reading drawings

## Views, and which one governs
- **Plan** (from above): wall-to-wall, the pieces of a top, seams, cut-out positions and offsets,
  sink centre lines, depths.
- **Elevation** (from the front): cabinet widths, fillers, heights above the floor, splashes, door
  and drawer layout, tags.
- **Section** (cut through): top thickness, overhang, edge profile, how the top sits on the box.
- GV reads the plan for cut-outs, offsets and wall-to-wall, the elevation for widths, fillers and
  tags, and the section for overhang. When two views disagree, that is a finding on the drawing
  that disagrees with itself; never pick one quietly.
- Each view has a title bubble (its name and number) and often a scale note. On GV's combined
  sheets the pasted drawings are often resized, so a printed scale note can be wrong: scale only
  from the view's own printed dimensions.

## How a dimension is drawn
- A **dimension line** with a tick, dot or arrow at each end, and the value printed on or beside it.
- **Extension lines** run from the object to the dimension line. The two outer ticks are what the
  dimension measures: follow each extension line back to the edge it starts from.
- Text on a vertical dimension usually reads from the bottom up.
- A **chain** is a row of dimensions end to end; the **overall** above or below it spans the whole
  row. The pieces must add up to the overall. A chain can be on the architect's drawing too, and the
  architect's own chain can be wrong.
- A **locating dimension** runs from a fixed edge (a wall, the floor, a cabinet end) to a thing's
  centre line. It is that thing's position. A thing the vendor leaves out is its own item.

## The three layers on a sheet
- The architect's drawing (the design), the vendor's drawing (what is checked), and GV's markup
  (coloured clouds, boxes, numbers and notes added by GV's reviewers). Drawings are plotted in black
  and grey, so anything coloured is GV's. A callout that ends "by GV" ("STONE TOP by GV") is GV's
  note, never the vendor's tag.
- A sheet can hold an ID-set drawing and a brand prototype side by side. Label each half, never the
  whole page.

## Rounds
- A later round has a new heading (rev 2) or a later date. The newest round is the vendor's current
  proposal: an item already corrected in round 2 is Fixed, not Wrong.

## Reading a value
- Feet and inches: `3'-4 1/2"` is 40 1/2". `660 [26]` is mm then inches; inches count.
- Stacked fractions (a small 1 over a bar over 2) are easy to misread: zoom in.
- Never type a value you cannot read. Name the label code numbered (D17) instead of typing it.
